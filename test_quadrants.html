Twelve Test Quadrants
Three parties decide what a test result means: the oracle (who says what the right answer is), the implementation, and the test. Each can be correct or wrong, and the oracle can also be absent. Hover or tap a quadrant to see the business scene it produces.
The grid
O oracle I implementation T test   correct   wrong ? absent (ambiguous) green red either

"Correct" for the implementation and the test means faithful to the oracle. With an absent oracle it means "agrees with the majority reading". The usual story about testing lives in one quadrant only: correct oracle, wrong implementation, correct test. The top-left quadrant is the one nobody looks at.

The running example

An online shop has a shipping rule. In the scenes, the real business rule is "free shipping at 10,000 yen or more". The oracle is the owner who tells the team the rule; the implementation is the developer's code; the test is the assertion written for the 10,000-yen order. The lamp is the CI result. What changes from quadrant to quadrant is who is wrong, and whether the lamp can tell you.
